Chef Bot: Semi-Automated Robotic Pancake Production System

Chef Bot was designed to reduce human interaction in the pancake-making process by integrating an Annin Robotics AR4 arm with custom mechanical subsystems for batter dispensing, flipping, topping application, and plating.

Project Overview

Building a robotic system to cook, flip, and plate pancakes

The Chef Bot company developed a human-assisted robotic pancake production system using the Annin Robotics AR4 arm and custom subsystems. The system was designed around a repeatable workflow: dispensing batter, cooking the pancake, flipping it on the griddle, applying toppings, and plating the finished pancake.

The project required coordination between mechanical design, dispensing, motion control, user interface, and physical layout teams. Each subsystem had to operate reliably while integrating into one complete robotic process.

System Workflow

  1. Operator fills batter and toppings.
  2. User selects order through the interface.
  3. Batter is dispensed onto the griddle.
  4. Robot arm flips the pancake using wrist rotation.
  5. Toppings are dispensed.
  6. Finished pancake is moved to the plate.

Project Goals

Design requirements and objectives

Reduce Human Interaction

Minimize manual involvement by automating batter dispensing, cooking motions, flipping, topping application, and plating.

Integrate Subsystems

Combine the AR4 robotic arm with custom mechanical attachments, dispensers, sensors, and a touchscreen interface.

Repeatable Process

Create a reliable sequence that produces accurate and consistent pancake results through controlled motion and testing.

Design

Mechanical subsystems and integration

End Effector

The end effector had to attach to the robot arm, work around the spatula required for flipping, and incorporate a cup attachment for dry toppings. The cup was redesigned with a larger back wall and smaller diameter to improve topping dispensing performance.

Oil Spray System

A servo-actuated sprayer was designed with an adjustable spray angle and quick bottle replacement. Testing later showed oil spray was not needed, reducing maintenance and removing a possible point of failure.

Calibration Stand

The stand was created to set up the robot arm in its home position, allow the arm to safely rest on the table, and reduce risk of damage during unpredictable motion.

Physical Layout

The complete layout integrated the AR4 arm, dry dispensers, spill plate, griddle, batter dispenser, plate sensor, and user interface into one coordinated pancake production system.

Testing and Validation

Improving reliability through iteration

01

Dispensing Tests

The batter dispenser used a stainless-steel funnel and servo actuation. A one-second actuation period was determined to dispense the desired batter amount.

02

Dry Topping Iterations

The dry dispenser slider required multiple design iterations due to bending and shear stress. The funnel and mounting brackets were also modified to improve actuation and fit.

03

Flipping Motion

The robot used slow J5 wrist rotation to flip pancakes, prioritizing accuracy over speed and reducing torque demand during the motion.

04

System Integration

Final testing connected robot motion, batter dispensing, topping dispensing, plating, and user interface feedback into a semi-automated process.

Results

Final outcome and future improvements

Goals Achieved

  • Semi-autonomously cooked, flipped, topped, and plated pancakes.
  • Integrated multiple custom mechanical and control subsystems.
  • Demonstrated repeatable robotic motion using the AR4 arm.
  • Created a user-controlled system with touchscreen interaction.

Next Steps

  • Add camera feedback for improved accuracy.
  • Expand order capacity beyond the current limit.
  • Program and install an autonomous reset system.
  • Continue reliability testing and mechanical refinement.
